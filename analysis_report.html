Executive readout

Spending is associated with longer lives, but the relationship is not linear.

  • The relationship is clearer after log-transforming spending per person, because health expenditure is extremely right-skewed across countries.
  • The fitted model suggests that each 10x increase in health spending is associated with about 9.6 additional years of life expectancy.
  • There is still meaningful variation around the trend line, which points to system efficiency, prevention, equity, income, and broader social determinants.
How to read this: dots above the regression line have higher life expectancy than expected for their spending level; dots below it have lower life expectancy than expected.

Methods

Reproducible analysis choices.

  • Data join: country-year records were matched where both life expectancy and total health spending per person were available for 2010.
  • Transformation: spending is plotted on a log10 scale to make low-, middle-, and high-spending countries comparable in one view.
  • Model: a simple ordinary least squares trend line was fit using life expectancy as the outcome and log10 spending as the predictor.
  • Limit: this is an ecological cross-country analysis. It describes association, not causation.
